# Engineering research

1. Define the question, decision, constraints, and what evidence would resolve it. Inspect relevant project context before recommending an approach.
2. Prefer the source that owns the claim: official documentation, specifications, release notes, or source code. Check the version and date where behavior may have changed. Open the relevant material rather than relying on search snippets.
3. When available and authorized, delegate independent reading with a clear scope while continuing useful work. Otherwise research directly. Review delegated evidence before using it.
4. Trace material claims to their source. Separate documented facts, observed project behavior, inference, and open questions. State when sources conflict or do not establish an answer.
5. Compare only plausible options against the user's constraints. Recommend the smallest suitable approach and explain material tradeoffs, prerequisites, and limitations.
6. Save one concise Markdown note in the project's existing research location, or choose and report a sensible location. Cite each factual claim near the text it supports; include the research date and the versions that matter. Keep sensitive material out of the note.
7. Summarize the decision and unresolved questions to the user. Research does not authorize installations, purchases, publishing, or unrelated implementation work.

Done when the decision has adequate evidence or the missing evidence is clearly identified, the cited note is saved, and the recommendation is grounded in the project's constraints.
